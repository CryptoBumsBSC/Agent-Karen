Dudley Bud and the Hydroponic Hijinks
"Hydroponics..." Dudley mused, tapping his chin with a slightly grimy finger. "Plants in water... No dirt? No mudslides? Count me in."

Featured Image
Dudley Bud squinted at the flyer pinned to the noticeboard at Evergreen Farm Trade School. It read: "Learn Modern Hydroponics: Field Trip This Friday!"

"Hydroponics..." Dudley mused, tapping his chin with a slightly grimy finger. "Plants in water... No dirt? No mudslides? Count me in."

“Hey, you reading that too?” came a voice. Dudley turned to see a lanky guy with messy hair and an oversized hoodie. The guy extended a hand. “I’m Marvin. Into hydroponics?”

“Uh, I’m Dudley. And yeah. Definitely. Love plants. Big fan of, uh, sustainable growth and such.” Dudley tried to sound professional, but the lingering smell of something herbal from his pocket wasn’t helping.

Marvin raised an eyebrow knowingly. “Cool, man. I’m looking to set up my own hydro grow someday. You?”

“Oh, yeah. Totally. Been my dream since, uh… a minute ago.” Dudley grinned.

Marvin nodded. “Field trip might be boring though. Wanna make our own plans?”

“What kind of plans?” Dudley asked, intrigued.

Marvin smirked. “We build our own hydro setup. Not just for lettuce and herbs, but, you know... herbs.”

Dudley’s eyes widened. “You mean… basil?”

“Sure, buddy. Basil,” Marvin replied with a wink. “But first, research. Let’s hit the library.”

The Great Library Expedition
The Evergreen Farm Trade School library was a cozy room full of books with titles like "Drip Irrigation for Dummies" and "The Lettuce Lovers’ Guide to Vertical Gardens." Dudley and Marvin plopped down at a table with a stack of books and a questionable bag of trail mix.

“Okay,” Marvin said, flipping through a colorful diagram of pipes and water pumps. “This one shows how to use PVC tubes. You can grow like fifty plants in a closet.”

Dudley squinted at the page. “But where do the fish go?”

“Fish?”

“Yeah, don’t you need fish? I saw a YouTube video about aquaponics. The fish poop, and the plants eat the poop.”

“That’s a different thing,” Marvin sighed. “Focus, man.”

“I’m focused!” Dudley protested, pulling out a crumpled notepad. “We’ll need... PVC pipes, a water pump, grow lights, and something called a nutrient solution. Oh, and a closet.”

Marvin groaned. “You don’t even have a closet?”

“Nope. Dorm’s too small. But…” Dudley’s eyes lit up. “What if we checked out a real hydro farm? Like, hands-on research?”

Marvin slapped the book shut. “Now that’s an idea.”

The Hydro Farm Adventure
The pair hopped on Marvin’s old dirt bike, which sputtered to life like a grumpy cow. They’d heard about a local farm a few miles away that specialized in hydroponics. The only problem? Neither of them thought to check if visitors were allowed.

“Are you sure this is the place?” Dudley asked as they rolled to a stop outside a large greenhouse surrounded by “No Trespassing” signs.

“Absolutely,” Marvin said confidently. “I Googled it last night. This is top-notch hydro tech.”

“But what about the signs?” Dudley pointed at the warnings.

“Relax,” Marvin replied, hopping off the bike. “They’re probably just for deer.”

“Deer can’t read,” Dudley muttered but followed anyway.

Inside the greenhouse, they marveled at rows upon rows of plants growing in neat, water-filled troughs. The air smelled fresh, and soft classical music played in the background.

“This is incredible,” Marvin whispered. “Look at that setup! Perfect lighting. Perfect nutrients. No dirt.”

Dudley leaned closer to one of the plants and sniffed. “I think this tomato just winked at me.”

“That’s the humidity messing with your head,” Marvin said, but he looked skeptical himself.

Suddenly, a loud voice boomed, “HEY! WHO ARE YOU TWO?”

Dudley froze mid-sniff. Marvin dropped his notepad. A burly farmer in overalls and a straw hat stormed toward them.

“Uh, uh, we’re students!” Dudley stammered. “From Evergreen! Big fans of, uh, sustainable... water… lettuce?”

“Yeah,” Marvin added. “We’re here for… inspiration.”

The farmer narrowed his eyes, then surprisingly broke into a grin. “Well, why didn’t you say so? I thought you were tomato thieves.”

“Tomato thieves?” Dudley whispered to Marvin. “Is that a thing?”

“No idea,” Marvin replied, equally baffled.

The Farmer’s Tour
To their relief, the farmer ended up being friendly. He gave them a full tour of the greenhouse, explaining how the pumps worked, how they balanced the nutrients, and how they kept the plants healthy.

“This is amazing,” Dudley said, scribbling notes. “But quick question… where do the fish go?”

“No fish, son. That’s aquaponics,” the farmer said, shaking his head. Marvin snickered.

As the tour wrapped up, the farmer handed them a small bag of seeds. “Here. Start small. Basil’s a good choice.”

“See?” Dudley whispered. “I told you. Basil.”

That night, Marvin and Dudley worked late into the night constructing their first DIY hydro setup. It was messy, uncoordinated, and leaked everywhere, but somehow, they managed to get a single basil plant growing.

“Hydroponics is awesome,” Dudley said, staring proudly at the plant.

“Totally,” Marvin agreed. “And next time, we’ll upgrade.”

Dudley nodded sagely. “Fish are definitely the next step.”

“Still not aquaponics!” Marvin groaned, but they both laughed as they admired their tiny green victory.
