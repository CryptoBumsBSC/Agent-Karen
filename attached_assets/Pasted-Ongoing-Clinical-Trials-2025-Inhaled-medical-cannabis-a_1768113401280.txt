Ongoing Clinical Trials (2025)

Inhaled medical cannabis and chronic pain
Large real-world trials are underway testing digitally controlled inhalers for chronic pain (nerve, PTSD-related, cancer pain). Results are pending but aim to provide broader effectiveness and safety data outside traditional settings.

Phase-3 trial on low back pain
A phase 3 placebo-controlled trial (full-spectrum extract) showed cannabis-derived medicine improved pain and sleep quality versus placebo in chronic low back pain patients — adding stronger randomized evidence.

💊 4. Population & Long-Term Health Studies

Brain function impact in heavy users
Large imaging studies show heavy lifetime cannabis use is associated with reduced brain activity during working memory tasks in young adults, suggesting functional impacts detectable in the brain.

Mental health and self-medication risks
Research indicates people who self-medicate with cannabis for symptoms like anxiety or pain have higher paranoia and psychotic-like outcomes compared to recreational users.

📉 5. Australian Research & Policy Insights

Australia 2025 report
A recent national report (Penington Institute) highlights ongoing health and safety concerns with cannabis, noting that many prescriptions occur despite limited clinical evidence, and regulator attention to product labelling and advertising remains high.

Need for reform
Experts in Australia are calling for urgent reform because policies and clinical guidance lag behind real-world prescribing practices, especially for chronic pain where evidence remains inconsistent.

🧠 6. Broad Academic Research Trends

Genetics & treatment response
Emerging cannabis studies investigate factors such as genetic polymorphisms (e.g., ABCB1) that may influence patient response to cannabis medicines — this could guide personalised therapies in the future.

Inflammation & oxidative stress
Reviews suggest CBD may modulate inflammatory pathways and nitric oxide synthases, offering potential rationale for research into inflammation-related conditions.

⚠️ 7. Health Risks Seen in Recent Evidence

Mental health associations
Systematic analyses conclude cannabis use is linked with anxiety, depression and suicidal tendencies, especially when begun in adolescence.

Cardiovascular risk signals
Some studies report associations between cannabis use and higher risks of heart attacks, stroke, and heart failure — emphasizing the need for careful medical assessment.

Cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome remains a concern
Severe vomiting syndrome (“scromiting”) is increasingly documented in chronic heavy users, underscoring that cannabis use is not free of gastrointestinal risk.

🧠 8. Key Takeaways for Patients & Clinicians
What current science really shows

✔ Strong evidence in a few specific conditions (e.g., seizures, chemo-related nausea)
✔ Growing clinical trials adding rigor (e.g., pain, inhalation devices)
✔ Emerging research on cannabinoids in cancer biology and inflammation

What remains uncertain

❗ Many common uses (anxiety, insomnia, general chronic pain) still lack high-quality evidence
❗ Long-term effects on brain function and mental health need ongoing study
❗ Risks (cardiovascular, psychosis, use disorder) are better documented as cannabis use expands
